The one-letter problem

Your own page sends orders to a Facebook handle that does not exist.

On cupcakesbysonjaonline.com/pages/cakeshop-by-sonja-custom-cakes, the paragraph that tells people how to order reads: “Order now via: Facebook: @cakeshopbysona · Instagram: @cakeshopbysonja.” The Instagram handle is right. The Facebook one is missing a j. We checked both against Facebook on 6 September 2026.

Printed on your ordering page

@cakeshopbysona

Facebook returns “Object with ID 'cakeshopbysona' does not exist” — error code 100, subcode 33. There is no page there. Anyone who types it exactly as printed reaches nothing.

Your real page

@cakeshopbysonja

Resolves immediately, with your cake-and-script profile picture attached — the same mark at the top of this page. This is the handle every link on this page uses.

Why this one is worth ten minutesIt is a single missing letter on the one line whose whole job is to take an order. It is also cheap to fix at the source: correct that sentence on your storefront page, and this page already carries the right handle everywhere it appears.

We checked it on 6 September 2026. The name resolves — it points at 205.186.175.89 — but nothing is listening there. Sixteen years of holding a name that currently shows a visitor nothing.
