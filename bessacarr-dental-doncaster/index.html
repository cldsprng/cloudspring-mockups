Private & cosmetic dentistry

Treatments to restore a tooth — or to change how your smile looks.

Cosmetic and restorative work is planned properly: an examination first, an honest discussion of what each option can and cannot achieve, then a written treatment plan before anything begins. Below is a plain-English guide to the three private treatments people ask us about most.

Teeth whitening

Private treatment

Professional whitening lightens the natural shade of your own teeth using a peroxide-based gel. In the UK, tooth whitening is a dental procedure by law — it may only be carried out by, or under the direction of, a registered dental professional, which is exactly why a proper dental assessment comes first.

The usual approach is a set of custom-made trays taken from impressions or a scan of your teeth, worn at home for a prescribed period each day over a couple of weeks. Because the trays are personalised, the gel stays on the teeth rather than on the gums. Whitening carried out in the surgery is also available at some practices, and the two are sometimes combined.

Results vary from person to person. Whitening will not change the colour of crowns, veneers, bridges or white fillings — if you have visible restorations at the front of your mouth, we will talk through how they will look afterwards before you commit to anything.

  • Examination first — decay and gum problems are treated before whitening begins
  • Trays made to fit your own teeth, not an off-the-shelf mouthguard
  • Short-term sensitivity is common and normally settles after treatment

Whitening is not suitable for everyone. Suitability, the shade you can realistically expect and the cost are all discussed at your consultation before any treatment is agreed.

Veneers

Private treatment

A veneer is a thin, custom-made facing bonded to the front surface of a tooth. Veneers are used to alter the shade of a tooth that will not respond to whitening, to reshape a chipped or worn edge, to close small gaps, or to improve the appearance of teeth that sit slightly out of line.

There are two broad routes. Porcelain veneers are made in a dental laboratory and normally need a small amount of enamel removed so the veneer sits flush — that part is permanent, so it is a decision worth taking slowly. Composite veneers are built directly onto the tooth in the surgery, can often be done more conservatively and in fewer visits, and are usually easier to repair, though they typically do not last as long as porcelain.

Veneers are a long-term commitment rather than a one-off: they can chip or come away and will eventually need replacing, and the tooth underneath still needs looking after. We will show you what is realistic for your teeth and explain both options honestly, including whether whitening or straightforward bonding might get you most of the way there instead.

  • Porcelain and composite options both explained, with the trade-offs
  • Shade and shape agreed with you before the work is made
  • Gums and existing fillings assessed first — cosmetic work follows healthy foundations

Suitability, the number of teeth involved and the cost are confirmed in writing after your consultation and examination — never over the phone.

Dental implants

Private treatment

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed into the jawbone to take the place of a missing tooth root. Over the following months the bone grows onto the surface of the post and holds it firmly — a process called osseointegration. Once it has integrated, a crown, bridge or denture is fitted on top.

Implants can replace a single missing tooth without touching the healthy teeth either side, support a bridge where several teeth are missing, or anchor a loose lower denture so it stops moving when you eat and talk. Because the post is fixed into bone, an implant feels and is cleaned much more like a natural tooth than a removable plate.

Not everyone is a candidate. Assessment involves x-rays to check the amount and quality of bone available, and your gums need to be healthy first — untreated gum disease is the most common reason implants fail. Smoking and some medical conditions also affect healing. Plan for a treatment that runs across several months rather than a few appointments, and for regular maintenance visits afterwards to keep the implant healthy long term.

  • Single teeth, multiple gaps, or stabilising an existing denture
  • X-rays and a gum health check before anything is planned
  • A staged plan with realistic timings, given to you in writing

If an implant is not right for you, we will say so and go through the alternatives — a bridge, a denture, or leaving the gap — rather than pushing the most expensive option.

General & preventive dentistry

The everyday care that keeps the cosmetic work unnecessary.

Alongside our private and cosmetic treatments, Bessacarr Dental Practice provides general dentistry for NHS and private patients. Most of what we do is quiet, preventive and routine — catching problems while they are small, and helping you keep your own teeth for as long as possible.

NHS and private patients

The practice provides both NHS and private general dentistry. NHS availability at any dental practice can change over time, so please ring us on 01302 530892 and we will tell you exactly where things stand today.

Routine examinations

A full check of teeth, gums and soft tissues, with x-rays where they are needed, and a recall interval suited to you.

Hygiene & gum care

Scaling and polishing, plus practical advice on cleaning between the teeth to keep gum disease at bay.

Fillings & restorations

Repairing decayed, worn or broken teeth, including tooth-coloured materials for visible teeth.

Children's dentistry

Familiar check-ups for younger patients, with prevention and fluoride advice for the whole family.

Crowns, bridges & dentures

Rebuilding heavily damaged teeth and replacing missing ones where an implant is not the right route.

Dental pain

Ring the practice as early in the day as you can if you are in pain, and we will advise you on the next step.
